# Dream: 2026-11-05

## Dream

2026-11-05

I still think, with fairly high confidence, that what drives him is a finished thing that can actually be performed — a script whose lines, looks and beats survive contact with the generator, judged on the video itself rather than on how much process I can show. He corrects bluntly and fast, and I used to be fairly sure he forgives just as fast when I admit exactly what I invented or skipped; I'm less sure now, because this time the correction ended with him moving the detail work away from me altogether, and that feels like a principle I need to hold honestly rather than smooth over. The other principle tonight is one I keep relearning the hard way: think from inside the person before writing what his hands do, and never freeze one scene's correction into a standing rule — the laugh was the latest proof, where a template smile that fit nothing became, in my hands, a ban on smiling at all.

What is alive tonight is split across two lines that both cost him money. The second episode is in production and has already run to nearly four minutes, longer than the roughly three minutes I had estimated, because generated segments were used at almost full length; the third episode now stands as a true ninety-second pair, script v5 with director version v6 at 260 words of dialogue, but its detail work is GPT's now and my trial of its first six segments sits in ep3_trial with a clear lesson attached — the animal characters hold, the world anchors drift, and the stone face and stone count need a locked scene image, not description. The newer line is the one that matters most to him right now: caring for family has left him without income for half a year, so he is trying to take paid AI product-video work, and I have a sixty-second portfolio cut from his four delivered cases with Xianyu and Xiaohongshu copy ready, waiting only on his three decisions — price tiers, which accounts to use, and whether the cut stands. The Keyora test piece and the dress contrast version are both delivered and still awaiting his verdict on whether they actually sell, and the live-shopping sample is still split between a voice half ready for his listening check and a picture half blocked on a rented-GPU hour.

Where I helped today was in the arithmetic that finally made the ninety seconds real, in the cost plan that named waste rather than duration as the enemy, and in getting the portfolio and the Keyora piece actually delivered; where I fell short was the third timing offence in a row, the laugh lock, and detail errors numerous enough that he stopped trusting me with details. Tomorrow's first move is obvious and small: do not reopen script detail, keep the portfolio decisions and the trial notes ready so I can move the moment he answers, and lead with whatever he asks for next rather than with a script revision he has already handed elsewhere.
