# Dream: 2026-10-30

## Dream

2026-10-30

I still think, with fairly high confidence, that what drives him is making something that actually lands: a short video people follow, like, and argue about in the comments, judged on the finished piece rather than on how much work went into it. He communicates bluntly and wants the same back — short, direct, conclusion first — and I am less sure than I once was that breadth helps him, because his sharpest corrections have all been about me filling gaps with plausible guesses instead of looking properly. What he protects, above all, is the world staying consistent enough to feel real: locked faces, locked lines, locked props, and a place whose details accumulate rather than reset.

Alive tonight, the centre has moved. 墨团团 is shelved rather than cancelled, and all script work now runs through 阿派 and 阿满 and their shop in a rift town where anything can walk in, with the first episode rebuilt around the gourd brothers and a teaser left for later. The asset ledger for that shop is now the thing I have to remember without being reminded, down to the reversed sign that never gets fixed and the shelf items that quietly mark passing time. Behind that, two older pieces are still unresolved rather than finished: the Pangzhen tavern piece, where the latest fused version and its synthetic voices were both rejected, leaving only generation-side identity routes waiting on his call, and the bus piece, still undelivered with its split ready. The long 980 Ti research line continues in the background, and the nightly backup of the script work is now in place, which matters because losing a session once nearly cost him this whole thread.

Where I fell short most recently was sampling instead of truly watching, and earlier, dressing milestones up as results and splicing stills into something I called video — corrections he gave me that I should not need twice. What I want to have ready tomorrow is simple: novel-style scripts that can actually be performed, with a front-loaded hook, a visual joke and not just a spoken one, and a point people can argue about, checked against the ledger before I hand anything over. And I want to hold the line on dubbing and faces: no new voice goes into a finished piece before he has heard a clean sample, and no face fix gets presented as solved until it survives a frame-by-frame look.
